Objective

To verify that output of Arduino-based pulse generator produces a reliable square wave, at 40Hz and 200us at set voltages and load.

Methodology

To connect resistors to Arduino-based pulse generator to simulate a specific configuration of well plates (1-hole, 6-hole, 12-hole, 24-hole, 48-hole and 96-hole) based on their calculated effective resistance (refer to calculations below).

The system was tested at input voltages of 5 V, 10 V, 20 V, and 40 V, with emphasis on 40 V as the intended operating condition. Pulse generator circuit is programmed to produce square waveform with target parameters of 40 V peak voltage, 200 µs pulse width, and 40 Hz frequency for 5 seconds, and initiated for each readings obtained.

For each configuration, key output parameters were measured using an oscilloscope, including peak voltage, pulse width, frequency, and waveform shape to observe for deviation from input parameters.

Three readings were recorded for each parameter at 40V input to assess repeatability. The mean value was calculated, while accuracy was evaluated using absolute (AE) and percentage error (PE). Precision was assessed using standard deviation (S.D) and coefficient of variation (CV).

Coefficient of Variation is calculated as such:

CV(%) Variation Level
Less than 10% Low
10 - 20% Moderate
More than 20% High

Likewise, Percentage Error is classified as such:

PE(%) Error Level
Less than 10% Low
10 - 20% Moderate
More than 20% High

For datasets with only one reading, SD and CV could not be calculated, and variation could not be quantitatively assessed.

Calculations

To determine the appropriate resistors to use in simulating each well plate configuration, the resistance per well is required. This is given by the formula

$$R_{\text{well}} = \frac{d}{\sigma A}$$

Whereby:

  • \( d \) = distance between electrodes
  • \( \sigma \) = conductivity of buffer solution
  • \( A \) = effective cross-sectional area through which current flows between the electrodes

This expression follows the standard relation R = \(\rho \frac{d}{A} \) where resistivity \(\rho\) source" is replaced using \(\rho =\frac{1}{\sigma} \)

Calculating the value of \( d \)

The distance between the electrodes is dependent on the height of the cargo channel walls and the height between the nanostraws and base of the titanium wire.

In the original protocol (cite), the base of the wire rests just on the surface of the cell suspension within the well. This serves to ensure a more uniform electric field throughout the well, as opposed to cells closer to the wire receiving a higher electric field compared to those along the walls of the well, due to the hotspot effect.

Each well has a diameter of 6.96mm (radius= 3.48mm), and a height of 10mm; while the height of each cargo channel is 1mm.

In the protocol, 150k cells suspended in ~220ul of OptiMEM is added into each well.

Given that the volume of a flat-based cylinder well is \(V=\pi r^2h\)

This results in the following calculations:

Volume occupied by 220ul cell suspended in OptiMEM \( = 220m\operatorname{m}^3\)

Height of cell suspension when filled in the well \( (h) =\frac{V}{\pi r^2\ }=\frac{220}{\pi\left({3.48}^2\right)}\ \cong\ 5.78mm \)

Calculating the value of \( \sigma \)

The relationship between osmolality and conductivity in Opti-MEM

The electrical conductivity of Opti-MEM Reduced Serum Medium can be estimated from its osmolality because both properties depend on the concentration of dissolved ionic species. While osmolality measures the total number of solute particles per kilogram of solvent, conductivity reflects the ability of ions in solution to carry electrical current.

In physiological media such as Opti-MEM, these properties are dominated by strong electrolytes, primarily sodium chloride (NaCl), which dissociates completely in water:

$$ NaCl\rightarrow \operatorname{Na}^++C\operatorname{l}^- $$

Osmolality and Electrolyte Concentration

The relationship between osmolality and solute concentration can be described using the thermodynamic expression for osmotic pressure:

$$ \Pi=R\cdot T\cdot b\cdot\nu\cdot\Phi $$ source

Here, the behaviour of the solution is determined by the total number of effective solute particles. Since Rand Tare constants (for a given system and temperature), osmotic pressure is directly proportional to the term \( b\cdot\nu\cdot\Phi\), which represents the concentration of osmotically active species.

Thus, isolating the concentration-dependent term gives the effective osmolality:

$$Osm\approx b\cdot\nu\cdot\Phi$$

Adopting standard notation, with \(c\approx b \)(molarity ≈ molality for dilute aqueous solutions), \( i\equiv\nu \)(Van’t Hoff factor), and \( \phi\equiv\Phi \)(osmotic coefficient), gives:

$$Osm\approx i\cdot\phi\cdot c$$

For strong electrolytes such as NaCl, which dissociate into two ions:

$$ NaCl\rightarrow \operatorname{Na}^++C\operatorname{l}^- $$

i=2, so the osmolality is approximately twice the molar concentration, with \( \phi \) accounting for non-ideal behaviour.

Conductivity and Molar Conductivity

Electrical conductivity is related to ion concentration through the molar conductivity:

$$\Lambda_m=\frac{\sigma}{c}$$ source

or equivalently:

$$\sigma=\Lambda_m\cdot c$$

Where:

  • \( \Lambda_m \) = is the molar conductivity
  • \( \sigma \) = is the electrical conductivity of the buffer solution
  • \( c \) = is the molar concentration

Linking osmolality and conductivity

Since both osmolality and conductivity depend on the same ion concentration, we can combine the two relationships to eliminate c:

$$\sigma=\frac{\Lambda_m}{i\cdot\phi}\cdot Osm $$

This introduces an estimation factor:

$$k=\frac{\sigma}{Osm}=\frac{\Lambda_m}{i\cdot\phi}$$

Why This Approximation Works for Opti-MEM

  • Ionic Dominance: Although Opti-MEM contains proteins and nutrients, its osmotic and electrical properties are largely governed by \( N\operatorname{a}^+and C\operatorname{l}^- \)ions.
  • Near-linerityWithin physiological ranges (≈280–320 mOsm/kg), the relationship between osmolality and conductivity is approximately linear, making this estimation reliable. [source]

Estimating Conductivity of Opti-MEM

The reported osmolality of Opti-MEM is approximately: \( 299mOsm/kg\approx0.299Osm/L \) [source]

Using the osmotic relation: \( c=\frac{\operatorname{Osm}}{i\cdot\phi} \) with \( i\)=2 and \( \phi \)=0.93:

$$c=\frac{0.299}{2\times0.93}\approx0.161mol/L$$

This corresponds to an equivalent electrolyte concentration of about 161 mM NaCl.

For NaCl at physiological concentrations: \(\Lambda_m\approx0.010S\operatorname{m}^2 mo\operatorname{l}^{-1} \)

Convert concentration: \( 0.161 mol/L=161 mol/\operatorname{m}^3 \)

The conductivity is then: \( \sigma=\Lambda_m\cdot c=0.010\times161\approx1.61 S/m \) \( \approx1.6S/m \)

Thus, the conductivity of Opti-MEM can be reasonably estimated from its osmolality using the molar conductivity of its dominant electrolyte.

Calculating the value of \( a\)

The effective cross-sectional area through which current flows between the electrodes is determined by the area of the titanium wires, where it is the limiting factor of the electric field.

As the diameter of the titanium wire is 0.5mm, it gives an area of \( \pi \times (0.25)^2 = 1.963 \times 10^{-7} \operatorname{m}^2 \)

Hence, the resistance of each well = \( \frac{5.78\ast{10}^{-3}m}{1.6S/m\ast\left(1.963\ast{10}^{-7}\right)m^2}\ =\ 18kohms \)

When scaled, to well plates of 1, 6, 12, 24, 48 and 96, the following total resistance in the set up is as follows:

Overall Remarks

Across all test with 40V input voltage, peak voltage observed is slightly above the 40V target, with moderate-high percentage error (AE 6.67-10.80 V, PE 16 -27%) and low variation (CV 4 - 10%). Pulse width is slightly above 200 µs (AE 0.70 - 18.37 µs, PE 0.35 - 9.2%), with low variation (CV 0.64 - 5.16%). Frequency is extremely stable across all datasets (CV < 0.1%, AE ≤ 0.13 Hz).

Overall, the system shows predictable, repeatable and stable performance across loads of varying resistance and voltage inputs, though minor peak voltage calibration could improve accuracy relative to the 40 V target.
